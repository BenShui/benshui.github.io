To analyze what drives the movements of long term yields, researchers always decompose it into two parts: (i) the expected path of short rates (ii) term premium - the compensation for investors holding long term bonds compared to the short term ones. There are infinitely many ways to decompose this. In the literature, researchers always apply (arbitrage free) affine term struture model to decompose the yields.  

From a central bank perspective, this decomposition is super appealing. The question is, does the model really give the measure of expectations that the Fed wants?
